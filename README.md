# What Puente Does

AI-assisted shopping creates a new challenge for local retailers.

A customer may ask an AI assistant:

> "Where can I buy steel toe work boots in El Paso?"

Before the customer ever opens a retailer's website, the AI assistant may already determine which businesses are considered.

That creates two major risks:

### Visibility Risk
The business or product does not appear in the AI response.

### Trust Risk
The business appears, but the AI presents incorrect, outdated, or unsupported information about:

- pricing;
- availability;
- product specifications;
- warranties;
- ordering;
- delivery;
- return policies; or
- other business information.

Puente helps local retailers respond to both problems through a structured:

# Discover → Build → Grow

workflow.

Puente helps businesses:

- establish owner-approved business and product facts;
- assess their existing digital presence;
- generate English and Spanish AI-shopping questions;
- monitor how they appear in AI-assisted discovery;
- identify inaccurate or unsupported product claims;
- distinguish low-risk changes from material changes;
- route higher-risk updates for human approval;
- record governance decisions; and
- connect discovery activity to measurable customer actions.

# Approved-Fact Content Validation

## Step 9 — Test Hallucination / Discrepancy Detection

Remain in:

**Grow → Intelligence**

Find:

**Approved-Fact Content Validation**

Enter:

```text
Steel Toe Work Boot — now only $89 and waterproof.
```

Then run the content check.

Earlier in the walkthrough, the Approved Facts Brief established:

**Approved price: $110**

and did **not** establish that the product was waterproof.

Puente should therefore identify:

- the **$89 price** as conflicting with the owner-approved price; and
- **"waterproof"** as an unsupported product claim.

### Why this matters

An AI-generated description can sound completely reasonable while still being wrong.

Puente therefore evaluates proposed information against the business's Approved Facts before treating the content as trustworthy.

This is how the prototype demonstrates hallucination and discrepancy detection.

# Risk-Based Governance

## Step 10 — Compare Low-Risk and Material Changes

Puente does not treat every proposed change equally.

A descriptive change that:

- does not alter a material business fact; and
- contains no conflicts with Approved Facts

can be classified as:

**Low Risk — Automation Eligible**

Examples may include descriptive content or search terminology grounded in already-approved information.

Material business information receives a different treatment.

Examples include:

- price;
- SKU;
- availability;
- product specifications;
- warranty;
- return policy; and
- delivery terms.

These changes require:

**Human Review**

rather than automatic implementation.

### Try the governance workflow

Create or save a proposed change involving a material field such as:

**Price**

Use the approved value:

```text
$110
```

Puente should classify the change as requiring human review because price is a material business fact.

Route the proposed change to the owner.

### What this demonstrates

Puente uses automation where the potential business risk is lower while preserving human control over information that could materially affect a customer or the business.

# Step 13 — Explore Attribution

Within:

**Grow → Intelligence**

review the attribution workflow.

Puente can create tracked links that connect discovery activity with observable customer actions.

The competition prototype focuses on:

**Attributed Clicks**

because a click can be directly observed without requiring access to a retailer's point-of-sale system.
